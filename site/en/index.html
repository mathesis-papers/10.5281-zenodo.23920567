Koljančić–Oblak Question 5.11 (arXiv:2604.21552v1, Section 5): nSSP witnesses for looped double paths

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

The manuscript asks when a chain with arrows in both directions between neighbors and selected self-loops supports a matrix with a strong spectral nondegeneracy property. Ma and Zhang give a complete existence criterion: an even number of vertices needs at least one loop, while an odd number needs a loop at an odd-numbered vertex.

Number the vertices consecutively from one endpoint. The matrix has nonzero entries exactly at arrows and loops; opposite arrows need not have equal weights. The non-symmetric Strong Spectral Property, or nSSP, excludes a hidden obstruction: a nonzero real matrix X that vanishes wherever the original matrix A is nonzero and whose transpose commutes with A, meaning their multiplication order does not matter. X need not be symmetric or have zero diagonal. The question connects prescribed zero positions with spectral behavior, without prescribing particular eigenvalues.

A pattern “allows” nSSP if some choice of weights works; it “requires” nSSP if every choice works. The authors answer Koljančić and Oblak’s even-order existence question, but credit Carptopus’s August 2026 work with previously proving both parity cases and the single-loop spectral characterization. Their contribution is an explicit integer construction, a unit-weight arithmetic test, and classifications for two loop families, not the first existence proof. Every allowing pattern has a symmetric witness with positive integer nonzero entries.

For one loop, deleting its vertex leaves two shorter chains, called branches. For a symmetric matrix with nonzero neighbor weights, the authors show that nSSP holds exactly when these branches share no eigenvalue. Consider five vertices with the sole loop at vertex three. Unit edges produce identical branches and fail. Giving the two edges on one side weight three, the other two weight one, and the loop weight one separates branch eigenvalues into ±3 and ±1 and succeeds. Thus allowing does not mean requiring. Generally, unit edges succeed exactly when the loop position and vertex count plus one are coprime, for any nonzero loop value.

The mechanism uses characteristic polynomials, whose roots are eigenvalues. Adding vertices recursively produces the branch polynomials, called continuants. Zero branch diagonals ensure that each contains only even powers or only odd powers. The authors vary the weights infinitesimally and ask whether the whole characteristic polynomial can remain unchanged to first order. The nonzero loop separates this equation into even and odd parts. Disjoint branch spectra mean their polynomials have no common factor; this forces both branch-polynomial variations to vanish. Working backward through the recurrence then forces every edge variation to vanish too.

To obtain nSSP for unrestricted X, the authors use the symmetric path matrix’s distinct eigenvalues. In eigenvector coordinates, any commuting matrix is diagonal. The differential calculation shows that edge-and-loop variations detect every diagonal direction independently. The zero-position restriction makes X orthogonal to these variations, forcing X to vanish. The construction achieves disjoint spectra by enlarging weights on an even-sized branch until its eigenvalues lie outside the other branch’s range. Additional prescribed loops are inserted with small positive weights: nSSP persists under sufficiently small perturbations. Scaling then clears denominators and gives positive integer entries.

The failures are structural. Without loops, the identity matrix obstructs nSSP. At odd order with loops only at even vertices, the excess of odd vertices supplies left and right zero-eigenvectors supported there; their outer product supplies an obstruction credited to Koljančić and Oblak. For odd chains of at least three vertices with only a central loop, existence holds exactly when the vertex count has remainder one upon division by four, but nSSP is never required. Loops at every interior vertex allow but do not require nSSP from order four onward; smaller orders do not allow it.

The all-orders conclusions rely on written proofs. The manuscript reports a Lean declaration for even-order existence with exact support and unrestricted real X, not symmetry, integrality, odd-order existence, the unit-edge criterion or failure constructions. Its joined-branch recovery formalization also assumes even total order. Reported exact-rational checks cover all loop sets through order eight, branch differentials through ten, all 78 single-loop positions through twelve, and interior-loop failures from three through twelve. These finite checks do not establish the infinite statements. The significance is constructive existence classification; the general classification of patterns requiring nSSP and the cited general minimum-arc requiring questions remain outside the settlement.
